# Annotated composition examples

Use the [technique index](art-direction.md#technique-index) to choose by story need, then open the linked image at its original framing. The descriptions below are textual annotations; originals are preserved unchanged. Eye paths are interpretations, and layer/motion suggestions are proposed adaptations.

## Inventory and source notes

Inspected 2026-11-12: all 47 JPEGs in the repository-root `references/` decoded successfully and were reviewed individually and in reduced, uncropped contact-sheet views. No byte-identical duplicates were found (SHA-256). All 47 remain usable as composition references. The filename suffix is the stable example ID; retain it when adding descriptive names.

Attribution for every entry: user-supplied files named `division48-studio-*`; most display a Division48 signature. Individual artists, original publication URLs, project assignments and reuse licenses were not supplied or verified. These are study references, not runtime artwork. The set includes fantasy, western and modern scenes; the earlier guide's blanket Witcher description does not identify every image reliably.

Related compositions: **5 / 9** are near-framing variants of the same commander gesture (wide sword versus tighter face/arm). **1v2 / 3v4 / 4v3** share a fighter but show distinct poses and framing. **2 / 3 / 5 / 9** share visual continuity, and **16 / 26 / 36 / 66** share winged motifs; neither group is a duplicate set. Names ending in `v2`, `v3` or `v4` are not duplicates of the corresponding plain number.

The annotations are available in every checkout. The image links require the original supplied files in the Git-ignored `references/` directory at the repository root. To restore the links in a fresh checkout, copy that directory from a workspace that has the originals and keep the filenames from the inventory. When the originals are unavailable, use the written annotations and record that the images could not be inspected. Keep the images outside `public/` and the runtime asset catalog. Black letterbox bands and signatures belong to the source framing, not the target 16:9 art. Recompose the picture area rather than stretching or blindly cropping it.

## Example 1

**Ruined village** — [Original image](../../../references/division48-studio-1.jpg). Source: supplied Division48-named reference; see attribution notes above.

- **Visible composition:** A wide street runs between broken huts; two animals tug at material below, with hanging silhouettes and a rider on the distant ridge. Dark fencing and branches crop the edges.
- **Visual hierarchy (interpretation):** The animal pair reads first in the lower opening, then the pale ridge reveals the distant figures. At small size the village silhouette survives better than the ridge details.
- **Technique:** [framing and atmospheric depth](art-direction.md#framing-and-atmospheric-depth).
- **Recap application (proposal):** Use an opaque ruined street, middle animal group, and near fence; ink the ridge lightly and keep a clear gap above the animals.
- **Limitations:** Do not make a crucial distant identity depend on the tiny silhouettes.

## Example 1v2

**Backward glance** — [Original image](../../../references/division48-studio-1v2.jpg). Source: supplied Division48-named reference; see attribution notes above.

- **Visible composition:** A blue-lit sword bearer twists toward the viewer; the blade exits left and the cloak sweeps to the lower right. The body is cropped at the hips.
- **Visual hierarchy (interpretation):** The pale face pulls attention before the hand and leftward blade. The glance and cloak mass survive reduction; facial wounds do not.
- **Technique:** [depth within one character](art-direction.md#depth-within-one-character).
- **Recap application (proposal):** Keep face and torso stable, derive a rear cloak and aligned hand-with-sword from one master pose over a blue-gray wash.
- **Limitations:** The blade leaves the frame; this does not establish a visible target.

## Example 2

**Leader facing the horizon** — [Original image](../../../references/division48-studio-2.jpg). Source: supplied Division48-named reference; see attribution notes above.

- **Visible composition:** An armored figure seen from behind fills the left half; a sword rises from the bottom right against a distant settlement and warm sky.
- **Visual hierarchy (interpretation):** Bright shoulder armor leads into the cape diagonal and toward the small sword. At small size the back-facing stance is clearer than the settlement.
- **Technique:** [landmark and scale](art-direction.md#landmark-and-scale).
- **Recap application (proposal):** Place landscape far, the figure in the middle, and a separate foreground sword only if it is not held by that figure. Use amber edges on muted ink.
- **Limitations:** The frame does not establish who holds the right-hand sword; avoid inventing a grip relationship.

## Example 3
